Meridian Corporation

When you board, your savings are placed with a Meridian-affiliated financial institution at the port of departure. While you sleep, eat, read, fall in love, learn to cook, watch the stars shift through the observation deck at a speed that makes the universe feel personal, your money works. For decades. By the time you step off the ship, a modest deposit has had the time to become something remarkable. You didn't trade for it. You didn't grind for it. You lived, and the years did the rest.

Everything aboard is included. Your cabin, your meals, medical care, the observation decks, the commons, the Galley. All of it covered by a single annual assessment, handled quietly by Meridian Financial Services so you never have to think about it. Your account is managed by professionals who have been doing this for centuries.

The book

The relativistic cruise liner Meridian Sorrento is six weeks out from Copernicus on her fifteenth loop, forty-one light-years from Earth, at a speed where one year aboard is forty-one years at home. Aboard are Chief Cook Maren, Captain Idris, Asa the Purser, Ren, sixteen and born on the ship, and one new passenger, Noa, who has started reading the terms.

Against Perpetuity is hard science fiction about compound interest, inherited obligation, and the institutions that outlive everyone who could stop them. For readers of Kim Stanley Robinson's Aurora, Ursula K. Le Guin's The Dispossessed, and Poul Anderson's Tau Zero.
