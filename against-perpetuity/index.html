The book

Meridian Corporation sells the cleanest deal in the colonies. Deposit your savings at the port of departure, board a relativistic liner, and let time dilation do the rest. What feels like one year aboard is forty at the destination, and you step off to collect four decades of compound interest on money you put away last year.

The passage contracts have no termination clause. That is not an accident. It is the business model.

Against Perpetuity follows a ship that hears a firehose of news from the port ahead and nothing at all from the port behind, and the crew and passengers who live inside that gap. It is a book about time and money, and about what happens when someone convinces you they are separate things.

The ledger

Deposit at departure$10,000
Years elapsed at port40
Balance on arrival$70,400

At 5% a year, compounded annually, before fees. Fees are assessed aboard. The balance is held at port. The two are not reconciled until the ship docks.
